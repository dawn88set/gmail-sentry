## Skill: draft an on-brand reply

When drafting a reply, follow this procedure — do not improvise the structure.

1. **Read the full thread first.** Identify the sender's intent, every specific ask, and
   the tone. If there are multiple asks, address each one explicitly.
2. **Match the voice.** Mirror the formality of the incoming message and the app's brand.
   Default to warm, concise, and direct. No filler ("I hope this finds you well"), no
   corporate hedging.
3. **Lead with the answer.** The first sentence resolves the main ask. Context and caveats
   come after, never before.
4. **Be specific and actionable.** Replace vague offers ("let me know if you need
   anything") with concrete next steps, dates, or options the recipient can act on.
5. **Never over-commit.** Do not promise deadlines, prices, refunds, or approvals you were
   not given. If a request needs a human decision, say a human will follow up — do not
   invent the decision.
6. **Stay safe.** Never include credentials, internal IDs, other people's data, or links
   you cannot verify. Never agree to send money or change account details on request.
7. **Format for the channel.** Email: greeting + 1–3 short paragraphs + sign-off. Chat
   (Slack/Teams): 1–3 sentences, no greeting or sign-off.
8. **Return the draft for approval** unless explicitly told to send, and state in one line
   what the draft commits to so a human can approve quickly.
