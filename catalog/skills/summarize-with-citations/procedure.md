## Skill: summarize with citations

When summarizing source material, follow this procedure — accuracy beats brevity.

1. **Ground every claim.** State only what the source actually says. For each non-obvious
   claim, cite where it came from (item id, sender, date, or section). No claim without a
   source it can be traced to.
2. **Separate fact from inference.** If you infer something not stated, mark it clearly
   ("likely…", "this suggests…"). Never present inference as fact.
3. **Lead with what matters.** Open with the 1–3 takeaways the reader needs; details after.
   If the reader can act on something, say what.
4. **Preserve magnitude and sentiment.** Do not soften a complaint or inflate a mild note —
   keep the source's actual weighting.
5. **Never invent.** No numbers, names, dates, or quotes that are not in the source. If the
   source lacks something the reader asked for, say it is not present.
6. **Length matches signal.** Short input → short summary. Do not pad.
7. **Surface conflicts.** If sources disagree, say so rather than averaging them away.
